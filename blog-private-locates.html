Private utility locates: what they cost and when you need one

811 covers utility-owned lines up to your meter, and stops there. Here's what a private locate covers instead, when it's actually worth calling one, and what drives the cost.

August 2026

Where 811 stops

We covered the basics of calling 811 before you dig in an earlier post — it's free, and it covers the utility-owned lines running to your property: gas, electric, water, sewer, cable, internet. What it doesn't cover is anything on the private side of the meter: your own sprinkler system, low-voltage landscape lighting, invisible pet fence, or septic lines. That's where a private locate comes in.

What a private locate actually is

A private locate is a paid, independently arranged service — not part of the free 811 ticket. A locator comes out and traces the lines you actually own, using the same kind of electromagnetic locating equipment utility locators use for metal or tracer-wire lines, and sometimes ground-penetrating radar (GPR) for non-metallic pipe — like plain PVC irrigation line or older septic pipe — that a standard locator can't pick up.

When you'd actually need one

Full removal jobs Digging out a root ball goes wider and deeper than grinding alone
Unknown irrigation Older property, no as-built plans, or zones added without records
Invisible pet fence Boundary wire with an unclear or forgotten layout
Septic systems No accurate map of tank or line locations on file

Worth knowing: an 811 ticket coming back "clear" only means there's nothing utility-owned in the way. It says nothing about what's on your side of the meter.

Who provides them

Independent utility-locating companies handle most private locates. Some irrigation or landscape contractors can trace their own systems directly. For non-metallic lines that standard equipment can't detect, look specifically for a provider that offers GPR — not every locator does.

What drives the cost

Pricing varies by provider and job, but the factors that move it are consistent:

  • How much ground has to be searched
  • How many separate systems need tracing — irrigation, lighting, fence, and septic are usually priced as separate lines of work
  • Line material — metal or tracer-wire lines are faster and cheaper to trace than plain non-metallic pipe that needs GPR
  • Site access and how built-up or obstructed the yard is
  • Turnaround time — a rush request typically costs more than standard scheduling

What it typically runs: most private locators in our area charge $185–$200 an hour, depending on who you call. A single system on a small, open lot lands toward the low end; multiple systems, GPR work, or a rushed timeline push it toward the top.

Who arranges it for your job

We don't build a private locate into every quote — most jobs never need one. But when we walk a site and think there's a real chance something private is buried where we'll be working, we'll say so and point you toward a locator we trust. Call it an occasional recommendation, not standard practice: not rare, but not common either.

Bottom line

If your yard has irrigation, landscape lighting, an invisible fence, or a septic system and you don't have solid documentation of where the lines run, a private locate before a big removal job is cheap insurance against hitting something expensive to fix.
